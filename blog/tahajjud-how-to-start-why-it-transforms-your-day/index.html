Tahajjud: how to start and why it transforms your day

Nuria · Aug 2, 2026 · 3 min read

What is Tahajjud?

Tahajjud—also called qiyam al-layl, the standing of the night—is a voluntary prayer performed after Isha and before Fajr, ideally in the final third of the night. It is not obligatory, yet Allah mentions it repeatedly in the Quran with unmistakable weight.

"And from [part of] the night, pray with it as additional [worship] for you; it is expected that your Lord will resurrect you to a praised station." (Surah Al-Isra, 17:79)

The Prophet said: "The best prayer after the obligatory prayers is the night prayer." (Muslim) He observed it so consistently that his feet would swell from the long standing, and when asked why he pushed himself, he answered: "Should I not be a grateful servant?"

What it does to your day

Those who pray Tahajjud regularly describe a quality of stillness and clarity that is hard to put into words. The house is quiet. There is no inbox, no notification, no one asking anything of you. It is just you and Allah.

That private conversation reshapes how you move through everything that follows. Decisions feel less anxious. Patience comes more readily. Gratitude arrives before frustration does. This is not metaphor—the scholars have always taught that a heart that meets Allah in the darkness is a heart that handles the daylight with more grace.

How many rakat, and how to structure them

There is flexibility here, and that flexibility is a mercy. Across the four madhabs—Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali—there is broad agreement that Tahajjud has no fixed upper limit and a minimum of two rakat. The Prophet typically prayed eight rakat in sets of two, followed by Witr.

  • Minimum: Two rakat—enough to begin and enough to benefit from.
  • Common practice: 8 rakat in pairs of two, then Witr (1 or 3 rakat, depending on your madhab).
  • Each set: Pray two rakat with Tasleem, then begin the next pair. Take your time in ruku and sujud—these are the moments to linger.

The best time to pray it

The final third of the night is when Allah descends, according to the hadith, and asks: "Is there anyone calling upon Me, that I may answer? Is there anyone seeking forgiveness, that I may forgive?" (Bukhari and Muslim)

If you sleep after Isha, set an intention and an alarm for the last hour or so before Fajr. If waking at that hour feels too ambitious at first, starting just after Isha—while still technically Tahajjud according to some scholars—is an honourable beginning. The goal is consistency, not perfection.

How to take your first step tonight

Start small and mean it. Two rakat with full attention is worth more than eight rushed ones. Here is a simple approach:

  • Make your intention before you sleep: "O Allah, help me rise for Your sake."
  • Set an alarm for 30 minutes before Fajr.
  • Perform wudu—the cold water wakes both body and heart.
  • Pray two rakat, reciting slowly. Let the ayahs settle before you move.
  • After Tasleem, sit and make dua in your own words. This is the moment the door is widest open.

Do this for one week without judging yourself harshly if you miss a night. What you are building is a relationship, not a record.

A final word

Tahajjud is not reserved for scholars or the especially devout. It is an open invitation, extended every single night to every single Muslim who wants to be known by Allah in the silence before dawn. Your first two rakat—however quiet, however imperfect—are heard.
