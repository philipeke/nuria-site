Tahajjud: how to start and why it transforms your day

Nuria · Aug 7, 2026 · 3 min read

What is Tahajjud?

Tahajjud is the voluntary night prayer performed after sleeping and before Fajr. Allah describes the believers who leave their beds to call on their Lord (Surah As-Sajdah, 32:16), and the Prophet ﷺ called it the best prayer after the obligatory ones. It is not an obligation—it is an invitation, and that distinction matters. You come to it freely, in the stillness before dawn, when the world is quiet and your heart has the space to speak honestly.

Why it changes everything

There is something that happens when you rise before the rest of the house stirs. Psychologists call it intentional solitude; Muslims have always called it the sweetness of the night. When you stand before Allah at that hour—asking, thanking, simply being present—the day that follows carries a different weight. Problems feel more proportionate. Patience comes a little more easily. The sense that you are not facing your life alone becomes less abstract and more felt.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Our Lord descends to the lowest heaven during the last third of every night, saying: Who is calling upon Me, that I may answer him? Who is asking of Me, that I may give him? Who is seeking My forgiveness, that I may forgive him?" (Agreed upon). This is not poetry—it is a standing invitation.

How to begin

Start with two rak'ahs

There is no minimum number that unlocks the benefit. Two rak'ahs performed with presence are worth more than eight performed while half-asleep. Set a gentle alarm for around 30–45 minutes before Fajr. Wake, make wudu, and pray two rak'ahs. Read whatever Quran is comfortable—even Al-Fatiha alone in each rak'ah is valid and beloved. Then sit for a moment and make du'a in your own words.

Consistency over quantity

The Prophet ﷺ said: "The most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if they are small." Two rak'ahs every night outweigh ten rak'ahs performed once and abandoned. Do not wait until you feel spiritually ready—that feeling often comes only after you have already shown up.

A note on the different madhhabs

Scholars across the four madhhabs—Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali—agree that Tahajjud is a confirmed sunnah. There are differences in how they count rak'ahs, how long one should sleep before it qualifies as Tahajjud, and whether Witr is attached or separate. Follow the guidance of your own tradition. What matters most is that you begin.

Making it sustainable

  • Sleep early. This is the most practical advice and the most ignored. An earlier bedtime is the single biggest factor in whether you actually wake.
  • Keep a glass of water nearby. Waking slowly and drinking before wudu can ease the transition from sleep.
  • Make du'a before sleeping that Allah wakes you. This simple act—asking Him to help you rise for His sake—has been the practice of the righteous across generations.
  • Do not judge nights you miss. Life has seasons. A missed night is not a closed door; it is simply an invitation for tomorrow.

The long view

Tahajjud is not a spiritual hack or a productivity ritual. It is a relationship—built slowly, through consistent return. The nights you drag yourself out of bed and feel nothing are part of it. The nights the words of du'a come easily are part of it. Over time, something shifts. You begin to notice that the first thing you want in a difficult moment is that pre-dawn quiet. That is what the companions and scholars meant when they spoke of Tahajjud as the light of the believer.

Begin tonight. Two rak'ahs. The door is always open.
