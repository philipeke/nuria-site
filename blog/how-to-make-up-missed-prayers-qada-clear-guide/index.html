How to make up missed prayers (qada): a clear guide

Nuria · Jul 29, 2026 · 3 min read

Life sometimes pulls us away from salah—illness, forgetfulness, hardship, or years before we fully embraced the deen. Whatever brought you here, the door of return is open. Making up missed prayers is called qada, and it is an act of sincere devotion, not of shame.

What is qada?

Qada refers to performing a prayer after its prescribed time has passed. It is distinct from ada (praying on time) and i'ada (repeating a prayer within its time). The obligation to make up missed prayers is established across all four major madhhabs—Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali.

Who must make up missed prayers?

A Muslim who missed obligatory prayers—the five daily prayers—is required to make them up, provided they were of sound mind at the time. The main scenarios include:

  • Sleeping through a prayer (with intention to pray)
  • Genuinely forgetting a prayer
  • Prayers missed during a period of illness where one was conscious
  • Prayers missed before returning to regular practice

Note: prayers missed during unconsciousness or severe illness (where one was not conscious) are treated differently across madhhabs—consult a scholar for your specific situation.

How to perform qada prayers

The method is straightforward. You perform each missed prayer in the same way as its regular form—the same number of rak'ahs, the same recitations—with one key difference in intention.

The intention (niyyah)

Make a clear internal intention that this prayer is qada. For example: "I intend to pray qada of Fajr from [date]." You do not need to specify the exact date in all madhhabs, but clarity of intention is agreed upon by all.

Order of makeup prayers

The Hanafi and Maliki madhhabs emphasise maintaining order (tartib) when making up a small number of missed prayers alongside current ones—meaning you pray the missed prayer before the current one if you remember in time. The Shafi'i and Hanbali positions consider order recommended but not strictly required in most cases. All scholars agree: do not delay making up prayers unnecessarily.

How to approach a large number of missed prayers

If you have many years of missed prayers, the task can feel overwhelming. Here is a gentle, practical approach:

  • Start now. Begin making up prayers today—even one extra prayer a day is progress.
  • Be consistent. Many scholars recommend praying one or two qada prayers after each current prayer, building it into your daily rhythm.
  • Keep count. Track roughly how many prayers you owe so you can work through them with intention.
  • Do not despair. Allah's mercy is vast. Your effort and sincerity matter deeply.

Can voluntary prayers replace missed obligatory ones?

No. Nafl (voluntary) prayers do not substitute for missed fard (obligatory) prayers. However, some scholars note that on the Day of Judgment, voluntary prayers may help compensate for any deficiencies in obligatory ones—but this does not remove the obligation of qada itself.

A word of encouragement

Turning back toward your prayers is one of the most beautiful things a soul can do. The Prophet  ﷺ  said: "Whoever falls asleep and misses a prayer, or forgets it, let him pray it when he remembers—for that is its time." (Bukhari & Muslim). Your qada is not a burden—it is a bridge back to Allah.
