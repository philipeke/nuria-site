How to pray when travelling: qasr and jam'

Nuria · Aug 5, 2026 · 3 min read

One of the most beautiful expressions of Allah’s mercy toward His servants is the way Islam accommodates human reality—including the reality of travel. When you are on a journey, two specific concessions exist to help you maintain your salah without hardship: qasr (shortening) and jam’ (combining). Understanding both is a practical act of worship in itself.

What is qasr?

Qasr means shortening the four-unit (rakat) prayers to two units. The prayers affected are Dhuhr, Asr, and Isha. Fajr (two rakat) and Maghrib (three rakat) are not shortened—they remain as they are.

The basis for qasr is the Quran itself: “And when you travel through the land, there is no blame upon you for shortening the prayer.” (Surah An-Nisa, 4:101). The Prophet ﷺ regularly prayed qasr on his journeys, and the companions followed his practice.

Conditions for qasr

  • A qualifying journey: The minimum distance differs by madhhab—roughly 48 miles (approximately 77 km) for the Hanafi, Shafi’i, and Hanbali schools; the Maliki school holds a similar threshold. Confirm with your own scholar if your journey is borderline.
  • Intention to travel: You must have made the intention to travel before leaving your city or town.
  • Not settled at the destination: If you intend to stay for four days or more at your destination (Hanafi: 15 days), you are considered a resident and qasr ends upon arrival.

What is jam’?

Jam’ means combining two prayers into one time slot. In practice, this means praying Dhuhr and Asr together, or Maghrib and Isha together. You may pray the first prayer early into the second’s time (jam’ ta’khir), or bring the second prayer forward into the first’s time (jam’ taqdim).

Fajr is never combined with another prayer—it stands alone at its own time.

Differences between madhhabs on jam’

This is where the schools diverge most noticeably:

  • Shafi’i and Hanbali: Combining prayers is permitted for a traveller meeting the qasr conditions.
  • Maliki: Combining is permitted for a traveller who is actively moving; once stationary, combining ends.
  • Hanafi: The position is more restrictive—jam’ in the conventional sense is generally not permitted for travel alone. Some Hanafi scholars permit a form of apparent combining (jam’ suri): delaying Dhuhr to the very end of its time and praying Asr immediately at its entry, creating the appearance of combination without actually combining in a single time slot.

If you follow the Hanafi madhhab and find yourself in a situation of genuine hardship, speak with a qualified scholar about what applies to your circumstances.

Practical guidance for travellers

  • Make your intention to travel before leaving home—this is the trigger for both concessions.
  • If you are praying behind an imam who is a resident, follow them fully: pray four rakat even if you would otherwise be shortening.
  • Keep track of your intended length of stay—the moment you exceed the residency threshold of your madhhab, full prayers resume.
  • In airports, on long flights, and at train stations, prioritize praying on time. Even a shortened prayer prayed on time is better than a full prayer prayed late.

Travel is a reminder of our dependence on Allah ۿ. The concessions He has given us are not loopholes—they are gifts. Use them with gratitude, and your salah on the road will be just as present and sincere as the one you offer at home.
