How to pray when travelling: qasr and jam

Nuria · Jul 27, 2026 · 2 min read

One of the beautiful signs of Allah’s mercy is that He did not leave the traveller without guidance. Whether you are on a long flight, a road trip, or moving between cities, Islam provides clear and gentle concessions to help you maintain your prayers without hardship.

The two main concessions are qasr (shortening the prayer) and jam’ (combining two prayers). Understanding these rulings—even at a basic level—can transform travel from a spiritually anxious time into one of ease.

What is qasr?

Qasr means shortening the four-unit (raka’at) prayers—Dhuhr, Asr, and Isha—to two units each. Fajr (two units) and Maghrib (three units) are not shortened, as they remain as they are.

The Quran explicitly mentions this concession: “And when you travel in the land, there is no sin on you in shortening the prayer” (4:101).

When does qasr apply?

  • Hanafi: The journey must cover approximately 77–78 km (48 miles) in distance, regardless of how long it actually takes. You begin praying qasr once you leave your city’s boundaries.
  • Maliki, Shafi’i, and Hanbali: The distance threshold is also approximately 80 km (2 marhalah), though scholars within each school vary slightly. You begin qasr once you depart from your town.
  • All four madhhabs agree that the intention to travel must be present and that you must not plan to stay at your destination for an extended period—though the definitions of “extended” differ.

What is jam’?

Jam’ means combining two prayers in one time slot. There are two forms:

  • Jam’ taqdim—advancing Asr into the time of Dhuhr, or advancing Isha into the time of Maghrib.
  • Jam’ ta’khir—delaying Dhuhr to be prayed in the time of Asr, or delaying Maghrib to be prayed in the time of Isha.

Madhhab differences on jam’

  • Maliki and Shafi’i: Combining prayers while travelling is permitted.
  • Hanbali: Combining is permitted for the traveller and also in situations of necessity such as rain or illness.
  • Hanafi: The Hanafi school does not permit combining prayers in time during ordinary travel. However, at Arafah (Dhuhr and Asr) and Muzdalifah (Maghrib and Isha) during Hajj, combining is established by the Sunnah and is permitted. Some Hanafi scholars also allow combining in extreme necessity.

Practical guidance for travellers

If you follow the Maliki, Shafi’i, or Hanbali school, you may both shorten and combine your prayers while on the road. If you follow the Hanafi school, shorten your prayers but pray each within its own time where possible.

The concessions of travel are a mercy, not a shortcut to be stretched beyond their purpose. Pray with presence and gratitude—even two focused raka’at on a crowded train carry the full weight of a sincere heart turned toward Allah.

When in doubt about your specific situation, consult a scholar from your own madhhab. The goal is always to pray with knowledge, not anxiety.
