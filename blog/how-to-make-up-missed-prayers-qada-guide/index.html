How to make up missed prayers (qada): a clear guide

Nuria · Jul 26, 2026 · 4 min read

What is qada?

Qada (قضاء) is the act of performing a prayer after its prescribed time has passed. In Islamic jurisprudence, every obligatory prayer (fard) carries a specific window of time. If that window closes without you having prayed—whether due to sleep, forgetfulness, illness, or any other reason—the prayer is still owed. It does not simply disappear. Making it up is itself an act of worship, a return to Allah with humility and intention.

This guide is for anyone who has missed prayers—whether a handful or many years' worth. There is no shame in beginning. The door is always open.

Is making up missed prayers obligatory?

The vast majority of scholars across all four major schools—Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali—hold that making up missed obligatory prayers is wajib (obligatory). This is grounded in the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ: "Whoever forgets a prayer or sleeps through it, let him pray it when he remembers it." (Agreed upon)

While there are minority scholarly positions, the mainstream and most widely followed opinion is that missed prayers must be made up, and this is the position you will encounter in nearly every school of thought and every recognised classical text.

How to perform a qada prayer

A qada prayer is performed exactly like the regular prayer—the same number of rakat, the same recitations, the same physical movements. The key difference is the intention (niyyah): you intend in your heart that this is a missed prayer you are now fulfilling, specifying which prayer it is (Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, or Isha).

  • The intention does not need to be spoken aloud—it is a matter of the heart.
  • You do not need to specify the exact date of the missed prayer, only which of the five prayers it is.
  • Qada prayers do not require a congregation; they can be prayed alone.
  • It is sunnah to maintain order (starting from the oldest missed prayer) when you know which prayers you missed and in which sequence.

What about Sunnah prayers—do they also need to be made up?

The rawatib (regular Sunnah prayers accompanying the five daily prayers) do not need to be made up in the same way. However, some scholars—particularly in the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools—recommend making up the Sunnah of Fajr if it is missed alongside the Fajr obligatory prayer, due to specific narrations from the Prophet ﷺ. For the other Sunnah prayers, the scholarly default is that they fall away if their time passes, and you move on.

What if I have many years of missed prayers?

This is perhaps the question closest to many hearts. If you have missed prayers spanning months or years—perhaps before you were practicing, or during a difficult period—the obligation remains. But the path forward is one of gradual, sincere effort, not despair.

A commonly cited approach among scholars is to:

  • Make a sincere estimate of how many prayers you have missed over how many years.
  • Begin making them up alongside your current prayers—for example, praying one or two extra prayers each day after your current obligations.
  • Maintain consistency, even if progress feels slow. Allah sees the effort and the intention.

Some contemporary scholars have discussed the concept of tawbah (sincere repentance) and its relationship to missed prayers, and there are different scholarly discussions around this. If you are carrying a heavy burden of missed prayers and feel overwhelmed, speaking with a knowledgeable scholar you trust—one who knows your circumstances and your school of thought—is a worthy step.

Can I pray qada at times when prayer is normally disliked?

There is a difference between the schools here. The Hanafi school generally holds that qada prayers should not be prayed during the three disliked times (when the sun is rising, at midday as it reaches its zenith, and when it is setting). The Shafi'i and Hanbali schools hold that obligatory makeup prayers may be performed at any time, including these intervals, because the obligation to make them up is not suspended. The Maliki school has its own nuance. If you are unsure, avoid the disliked times when you can—but do not let uncertainty become an excuse to delay.

A gentle reminder

The fact that you are thinking about qada is itself meaningful. Allah subhanahu wa ta'ala is Al-Ghafur, Al-Rahim—the Oft-Forgiving, the Most Merciful. Every prayer you make up is a conversation reopened, a debt honoured, a step taken toward the One who has never stopped waiting for you.

Start today. Start with one prayer. And let that one prayer lead to the next.
