Tahajjud: how to start and why it transforms your day

Nuria · Jul 26, 2026 · 4 min read

What is tahajjud?

Tahajjud—also called qiyam al-layl, the standing of the night—is a voluntary prayer performed after Isha and before Fajr, ideally in the last third of the night. It is not obligatory, but the Quran and the Sunnah speak of it with a weight that few other voluntary acts carry.

Allah says in Surah Al-Isra (17:79):

"And from [part of] the night, pray with it as additional [worship] for you; it is expected that your Lord will resurrect you to a praised station."

That phrase—a praised station—is understood by scholars across all madhhabs to refer to the highest intercession on the Day of Judgement. This is not a small promise.

Why the last third of the night?

The Prophet  saw  told us that Allah descends—in a manner befitting His majesty—to the lowest heaven during the final third of every night and calls out: "Who is supplicating to Me, that I may answer him? Who is asking of Me, that I may give him? Who is seeking My forgiveness, that I may forgive him?" (Bukhari and Muslim)

This is the window. The world is asleep, your phone is quiet, and there is nothing between you and your Lord. The scholars describe this time as uniquely open for du'a, for weeping, for honesty—for the kind of conversation you cannot always have in the rush of the day.

How many rak'ahs, and how do you pray them?

There is flexibility here, and this is a mercy. The Prophet  saw  prayed tahajjud in various numbers—sometimes eight rak'ahs followed by witr, sometimes fewer. The scholars of all four madhhabs agree on the following:

  • Minimum: one rak'ah of witr counts as a valid night prayer, though two rak'ahs of tahajjud followed by one rak'ah of witr is a common starting point.
  • Recommended: two rak'ahs at a time, with salam after each pair, ending with witr (one or three rak'ahs, depending on your madhhab's guidance).
  • Maximum: there is no fixed upper limit for voluntary prayer at night.

Recite slowly. This is not a prayer to rush. Choose surahs you know well enough to reflect on—even if it is only Al-Ikhlas and Al-Falaq repeated. The quality of presence matters far more than the length.

Practical steps to begin tonight

The biggest barrier to tahajjud is not willpower—it is waking up. Here is a simple way to start:

  • Set your intention before sleep. A sincere niyyah is itself an act of worship. The scholars note that whoever intends to wake for qiyam and sleeps through it may still receive its reward if they were genuinely prevented.
  • Sleep early. Even an hour earlier creates the conditions for waking. The Prophet  saw  discouraged unnecessary conversation after Isha—that guidance makes more sense when you understand what the last third of the night holds.
  • Set an alarm for the last third. Calculate when Fajr is, count back a third of the total night, and aim for that window. Apps like Nuria show you prayer times and can help you identify this window in your city.
  • Start with two rak'ahs. Do not wait until you feel ready to pray eight. Two sincere rak'ahs that you actually pray are worth more than eight you keep meaning to pray.
  • Make du'a after your salah. This is the moment scholars describe as most beloved for supplication. Speak to Allah in your own language, about whatever is in your heart.

What changes when you pray tahajjud?

Those who have built a consistent tahajjud practice describe something difficult to put into words: a kind of clarity that settles over the day. Problems that felt impossible at midnight look different after you have stood before Allah in the dark and asked for His guidance.

There is also a practical dimension. Waking before Fajr means you are already up for the morning prayer—one of the most virtuous prayers of the day—without the scramble of an alarm. Your day begins with worship rather than obligation.

But the deepest change is relational. Tahajjud builds a private closeness with Allah that communal and obligatory worship, as important as those are, cannot fully replicate. It is the prayer of those who choose to come—not because the adhan called them, but because they wanted to.

A note on consistency over intensity

The Prophet  saw  said: "The most beloved deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if they are small." (Bukhari and Muslim)

Do not begin tahajjud by aiming to pray every night for the rest of your life. Begin by praying it tonight. Then tomorrow night. Build the habit the way the Prophet  saw  described—steadily, without excess that burns you out. Even twice a week, maintained for years, will reshape you in ways you cannot predict from the outside.

Start tonight. Two rak'ahs. Before Fajr. The door is open.
