What is Witr prayer and when to pray it

Nuria · Aug 6, 2026 · 3 min read

What is Witr prayer?

Witr—meaning odd number in Arabic—is a prayer performed after the obligatory Isha prayer. It ends the night's prayers on an odd number of rak'ahs, and the Prophet ♟ was so consistent in performing it that scholars have long considered it among the most important of all voluntary acts of worship.

The Prophet ♟ said: "Make Witr your last prayer of the night." (Bukhari and Muslim). This simple instruction carries something of the spirit of Witr: it is a closing, a seal—a way of ending your day in remembrance of Allah.

Is Witr obligatory or voluntary?

This is one of the genuine points of difference between the madhhabs, and it is worth knowing where your tradition stands.

  • Hanafi: Witr is wajib—a confirmed obligation, one level below fard. Leaving it without excuse is sinful, and a makeup (qada) is required if missed.
  • Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali: Witr is a sunnah mu'akkadah—a strongly recommended practice. Neglecting it habitually is blameworthy, though it does not carry the same ruling as leaving a fard act.

Whichever position you follow, the scholars are unanimous that Witr is not something to be treated lightly. The Prophet ♟ never left it, even when traveling.

When should you pray Witr?

The time for Witr begins after Isha and ends at the break of Fajr. Within that window, you have flexibility—but there is wisdom in how you use it.

If you are confident you will wake for Tahajjud in the last third of the night, it is better to delay Witr until then, praying it after your night prayers. The last third of the night is a time of special closeness to Allah, and Witr at that time carries a particular depth.

If you are not sure you will wake, pray Witr before sleeping. This was explicitly advised by the Prophet ♟ for those who fear they will not rise. It is better to secure your Witr than to risk missing it entirely.

How many rak'ahs is Witr?

The minimum is one rak'ah. The Prophet ♟ said: "Witr is one rak'ah at the end of the night." (Muslim). Most commonly, people pray three, five, seven, or eleven rak'ahs.

Again, the madhhabs differ in the detail of how multiple rak'ahs are structured—particularly whether three rak'ahs are prayed with one tashahhud or two, and whether they should be joined or separated. If you follow a specific madhhab, the practical guidance from a scholar in your tradition is the place to look for the method.

The Du'a al-Qunut in Witr

A beautiful feature of Witr—particularly in the Hanbali and Shafi'i traditions—is the Qunut supplication recited in the final rak'ah before or after ruku'. In the Hanafi tradition, Qunut is recited in every Witr prayer as a wajib act. Maliki scholars generally hold that Qunut in Witr is not established.

The Qunut of Witr is an opportunity to ask Allah directly, in the quiet of the night, for guidance, forgiveness, and well-being. Even if it is only the brief supplication the Prophet ♟ taught his grandson: "O Allah, guide me among those You have guided..."

A prayer worth protecting

Witr is not a heavy obligation. It takes a few minutes. But those minutes—at the end of the day, before sleep or after the stillness of the night—carry a quality that is hard to find in any other time. The Prophet ♟ loved it. That alone is reason enough to make it a habit worth protecting.
