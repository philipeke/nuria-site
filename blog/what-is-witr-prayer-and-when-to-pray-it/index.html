What is Witr prayer and when should you pray it

Nuria · Jul 30, 2026 · 3 min read

What is Witr prayer?

Witr—meaning odd number in Arabic—is a voluntary prayer performed after the Isha (night) prayer and before Fajr. It is prayed in an odd number of units: 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9 rakaat, depending on the madhhab and personal practice.

The Prophet ﷺ never abandoned Witr—whether at home or travelling—which is why scholars across all four madhhabs hold it in high regard, even as they differ on its exact ruling.

Is Witr obligatory or voluntary?

This is one of the points where the madhhabs differ, and both positions deserve respect:

  • Hanafi madhhab: Witr is wajib (strongly obligatory)—one of the most binding categories of act. Leaving it deliberately is sinful in this view.
  • Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali madhhabs: Witr is a confirmed Sunnah (sunnah mu'akkadah)—highly recommended and strongly disliked to leave, but not in the same obligatory category as the five daily prayers.

Whichever view your tradition follows, the scholars are united that Witr is not something to take lightly.

When should you pray Witr?

Witr begins after you have prayed Isha and ends at the break of Fajr. Within that window, there is flexibility:

  • If you plan to wake for Tahajjud, it is better to delay Witr until after your night prayers—making it the last prayer before Fajr.
  • If you are not confident you will wake, pray Witr before sleeping. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever fears he will not wake at the end of the night, let him pray Witr at the beginning of it." (Muslim)

There is no shame in praying Witr early. The goal is consistency—never missing it—not a performance of endurance.

How many rakaat is Witr?

The minimum is one raka'ah, though most scholars recommend at least three. Common practices include:

  • Three rakaat—the most widely practiced form, either as two then one (with a salam between) or as three continuous.
  • Eleven rakaat—reported from the Prophet ﷺ during Ramadan, often performed as four, four, and three.

Follow the guidance of your madhhab and your local scholars. The spirit of Witr is to end your night in worship—the exact number is secondary to the sincerity you bring.

The du'a al-Qunut in Witr

In the Hanafi tradition, du'a al-Qunut is recited in every Witr prayer, in the third raka'ah before ruku'. In the Hanbali and Shafi'i traditions, Qunut is recited in the second half of Ramadan or year-round, after ruku' in the final raka'ah. The Maliki tradition recites Qunut in Fajr rather than Witr.

These differences are a mercy—not a source of division. Learn the du'a, understand its meaning, and let it be a moment of genuine conversation with Allah.

A gentle reminder

The Prophet ﷺ described Witr as a right that Allah has placed on you. To close the night—however ordinary—with an odd number of rakaat and a sincere heart is to carry something of value into your sleep. Don't let perfect be the enemy of present. One raka'ah, prayed with full attention, is more beloved than a longer prayer prayed in haste.
