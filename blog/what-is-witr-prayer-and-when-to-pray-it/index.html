What is Witr prayer and when should you pray it

Nuria · Jul 26, 2026 · 4 min read

What is Witr?

The word witr means odd—a single, solitary number that stands apart. In the Sunnah, the Prophet ﷺ described Allah as witr, One who loves what is odd, and he encouraged the ummah to end their night prayer on an odd number of rak'ahs. That final odd prayer became known simply as Witr.

Witr is a confirmed prayer performed at night, between the Isha prayer and the beginning of Fajr. It is the seal on your night—a moment to turn toward Allah before sleep takes you, or before the light of dawn calls you back to the fard.

What is the ruling on Witr?

The scholars across the four madhhabs are united that Witr is of the highest importance, though they differ on its exact classification.

  • Hanafi: Witr is wajib—obligatory, sitting just below fard in rank. Leaving it intentionally without excuse is sinful, and it must be made up if missed.
  • Maliki, Shafiʿi, and Hanbali: Witr is a sunnah muʿakkadah—a strongly confirmed Sunnah. Abandoning it habitually is considered blameworthy. The Maliki madhhab places particular emphasis on it.

Wherever you follow, the message is the same: this is not a prayer to skip lightly.

When should you pray Witr?

The time for Witr begins after you have prayed Isha and extends until the true dawn of Fajr enters. You have the whole night.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has blessed you with an extra prayer, which is better for you than the red camels: it is Witr, and He has placed it for you between Isha and the appearance of dawn." (Tirmidhi)

If you intend to wake for Tahajjud or Qiyam al-Layl, it is better to delay Witr to the last portion of the night, sealing your standing with Allah on that odd rak'ah before Fajr comes in. The Prophet ﷺ encouraged this, saying the best time is the last third of the night.

However, if you are not confident you will wake—if sleep tends to carry you through to Fajr—then pray Witr before you sleep. The Prophet ﷺ advised his companion Abu Hurairah, who could not be sure of waking, to pray Witr early. This is a concession rooted in kindness, not a lesser choice.

How many rak'ahs is Witr?

Witr must be an odd number. The minimum is one rak'ah, and the maximum documented in the Sunnah is eleven.

The most common practices are:

  • One rak'ah—the bare minimum, valid according to the majority. Hanafi scholars consider this insufficient on its own for a complete Witr.
  • Three rak'ahs—the most widely practiced form. In the Hanafi school, these are prayed as two rak'ahs followed by one more with a single tasleem at the end. In the other madhhabs, many scholars recommend praying two rak'ahs with tasleem, then one rak'ah alone.
  • Five, seven, nine, or eleven rak'ahs—all established in the Sunnah for those who want to lengthen their night prayer.

Duʿa al-Qunut in Witr

It is Sunnah to recite Duʿa al-Qunut in the final rak'ah of Witr—raising your hands and asking Allah for guidance, wellbeing, and forgiveness. The Hanafi school recites Qunut before the rukuʿ of the final rak'ah. The Shafiʿi and Hanbali schools, and many Maliki scholars, recite it after rising from rukuʿ.

The Prophet ﷺ taught a simple and profound Qunut: "Allahumma ihdini fiman hadayt…"—O Allah, guide me among those You have guided. If you do not yet know it by heart, any sincere supplication in that moment carries the spirit of what is asked.

Witr in Ramadan

During Ramadan, Witr is prayed in congregation after Tarawih in most mosques. This is an established practice from the time of Umar ibn al-Khattab, who gathered the people for Tarawih and concluded with Witr together. If you pray Witr with the imam in Tarawih, you do not need to repeat it at night—unless you intend to pray Qiyam after, in which case some scholars advise leaving Witr until the end of your night prayer.

A gentle reminder

Witr is one of those prayers that marks the character of a believer's night. It need not be long. One rak'ah, quietly and sincerely offered, closes the day on a note of remembrance. Begin with where you are, and let the practice grow from there.
