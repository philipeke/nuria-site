What is Witr prayer and when to pray it

Nuria · Aug 4, 2026 · 3 min read

What is Witr?

Witr—meaning 'odd number' in Arabic—is a prayer offered in an odd number of rak'ahs, typically performed after Isha and before the Fajr adhan. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Make Witr the last of your night prayers." (Bukhari, Muslim)

It holds a unique place in Islamic worship: something the Prophet ﷺ was deeply consistent about, even while travelling—a practice that sets it apart from most other voluntary prayers.

Is Witr obligatory or voluntary?

This is one of the areas where the madhhabs differ, and each position has strong grounding.

  • Hanafi: Witr is wajib—obligatory in a degree below fard. Leaving it without excuse is sinful according to this view. It is prayed as three rak'ahs with one tashahhud and one salam at the end.
  • Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali: Witr is a confirmed sunnah (sunnah mu'akkadah)—strongly recommended but not obligatory. Leaving it regularly is considered blameworthy. It may be prayed as one rak'ah or more, ending on an odd number.

Regardless of which position you follow, the consistent message from scholars across all schools is: do not neglect Witr.

When should you pray Witr?

The time for Witr begins after Isha and ends with the arrival of Fajr. Within that window, you have two main approaches:

  • Pray it before sleeping—this is recommended for those who are not confident they will wake for Tahajjud or the last third of the night.
  • Delay it to the end of the night—if you regularly wake for night prayer, this is the more virtuous option. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever fears he will not rise at the end of the night, let him make Witr at the beginning of it. And whoever is confident of rising at the end, let him make Witr at the end—for the prayer of the end of the night is witnessed, and that is more virtuous." (Muslim)

How many rak'ahs is Witr?

This depends on your madhhab and personal practice:

  • The minimum is one rak'ah (Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali view).
  • Common lengths are three, five, seven, or nine rak'ahs.
  • The Hanafi school prescribes three rak'ahs as wajib.

If praying three rak'ahs, the Hanafi school performs them as one unit with a single salam, while Shafi'i and Hanbali scholars often recommend two rak'ahs followed by one separate rak'ah, to distinguish it from the Maghrib prayer.

The du'a al-Qunut in Witr

Many Muslims recite a special supplication—Qunut—in the final rak'ah of Witr, typically after ruku' or before it. This practice is confirmed in hadith and widely observed, though the specific timing varies by madhhab. The well-known Qunut taught by the Prophet ﷺ begins: Allahumma ihdini fiman hadayt…

What if you miss Witr?

If Witr is missed, it can be made up as a qada (makeup) prayer during the day, though it is prayed in an even number of rak'ahs by some scholars to distinguish it from the nighttime odd. The Hanafi school holds it must be made up; the other schools consider it a sunnah that can simply be made up in its original form.

The key takeaway: Witr is a prayer worth protecting. Even on the busiest nights, one rak'ah before sleeping keeps the sunnah alive—and that is a beautiful thing to carry with you.
