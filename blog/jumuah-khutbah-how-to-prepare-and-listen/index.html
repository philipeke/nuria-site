The Jumu'ah khutbah: how to prepare and listen

Nuria · Jul 31, 2026 · 3 min read

Every Friday, Muslims around the world pause their week, gather in the masjid, and sit before the khatib. The khutbah has been part of Jumu'ah since the time of the Prophet ﷺ—it is not an opening act for the prayer but a pillar of the prayer itself. Yet many of us arrive distracted, leave without remembering a word, and miss the opportunity that is right in front of us.

That does not have to be the routine. With a little intention beforehand and a few things to listen for, Jumu'ah can become one of the most grounding moments of your week.

Before you arrive

The Sunnah of Friday begins the night before—or at least from the moment you wake up on that morning. A few things that set the right tone:

  • Ghusl and clean clothes. All four madhabs consider the ghusl of Friday Sunnah muakkadah. It is not just hygiene—it is an intention that this day is different.
  • Recite Surah Al-Kahf. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites it on Friday will have a light between two Fridays. Read it in the morning if you can, or on the way.
  • Send salawat abundantly. Friday is the day on which your salawat upon the Prophet ﷺ are presented to him directly.
  • Arrive early. The earlier you arrive, the closer you are to the front, the quieter your mind when the khutbah begins, and the greater your reward. Those who arrive very early are like those who offered a camel in sadaqah—so the hadith reminds us.

During the khutbah—what to listen for

The khutbah has a structure. Once you recognise it, you will find it easier to follow and absorb.

The opening and the reminder

Most khutbahs open with hamd, tashahud, and a verse or two of Quran. This is not formality—the khatib is setting the theme. Pay attention to which ayah is chosen. It will usually anchor everything that follows.

The core message

Listen for one central idea, not ten. A good khatib plants a single seed. Ask yourself: what is the one thing this khutbah is asking me to change or reflect on? If you can answer that question by the time you stand for prayer, the khutbah has done its work.

The Quranic and hadith references

When the khatib quotes from the Quran or the Sunnah, lean in. These are the foundations of the argument. Even if your Arabic is limited, the translation—whether given by the khatib or looked up later—is worth seeking out.

The du'a

The second khutbah traditionally ends with a du'a for the ummah—for those who are suffering, for the deceased, for the community gathered in the masjid. This is a moment of collective sincerity. Raise your hands internally even if your hands are in your lap.

What the scholars say about listening

Across all madhabs, there is consensus that speaking during the khutbah—even to tell someone else to be quiet—is impermissible or at the very least strongly disliked. The Hanbali and Shafi'i positions are particularly strict on this. This is not mere etiquette; it reflects the weight the Sunnah places on attentiveness.

Put your phone away. Not on silent—away. The khutbah is twenty minutes. The rest of the world can wait.

After the prayer—make it stick

Before you leave the masjid or immediately after, take thirty seconds and write down the one thing you want to carry from that khutbah into your week. It does not need to be elaborate. It might be a single name of Allah you want to reflect on, a verse you want to memorise, or one behaviour to adjust. Small and consistent beats grand and forgotten.

Jumu'ah is a weekly reset. Treat it like one—and you will find that over time, it quietly reshapes you.
