The Jumuʿah khutbah: how to prepare and listen

Nuria · Aug 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Why the khutbah deserves your full attention

Allah has gathered the believers every Friday around a single address. The khutbah is not background noise before the prayer—it is the centrepiece, a reminder delivered on the day the Prophet ۷ called the best day upon which the sun has risen. Scholars across all four madhhabs agree that listening attentively is an obligation once the imam begins, and that even gesturing to someone else to be quiet breaks the reward of the one who gestures. That is how seriously the tradition treats this moment.

Yet many of us arrive distracted, scroll our phones in the car beforehand, and sit in the rows thinking about work. The khutbah washes over us without landing. A little intentional preparation changes that entirely.

How to prepare before you arrive

Make ghusl and dress with care

The Prophet ۷ strongly encouraged ghusl on Friday—many scholars consider it a confirmed sunnah. Wearing your best clean clothes is not vanity; it is a physical expression that this time matters. The body and the spirit prepare together.

Recite Surah al-Kahf

Reading Surah al-Kahf on Friday, in full or in part, carries tremendous virtue reported in multiple sound hadiths. Beyond the reward, the surah itself—with its stories of the People of the Cave, of Musa and al-Khidr, of Dhul-Qarnayn—trains the heart in reliance on Allah and clarity of purpose. It is a beautiful way to arrive at Jumuʿah already spiritually awake.

Send abundant salawat on the Prophet ۷

Friday is the day of salawat. Fill the gaps in your morning—the commute, the minutes waiting—with sending blessings on the Prophet ۷. It softens the heart and connects you to why Jumuʿah exists.

Arrive early

The hadith comparing the reward of those who arrive at different times—like one who brings a camel, a cow, a sheep—is a gentle encouragement, not a competition. Arriving early means you pray the sunnah prayers, settle into stillness, and enter the khutbah calm rather than breathless.

What to listen for during the khutbah

The opening praise and reminder of taqwa

A valid khutbah opens with praise of Allah, testimony of faith, and blessings on the Prophet ۷. Scholars note that the imam’s reminder to have taqwa—God-consciousness—is the beating heart of every khutbah. When you hear it, let it land. Ask yourself quietly: where is my taqwa thin this week?

The Quranic ayah or hadith

Most imams anchor their khutbah in a verse or a prophetic narration. This is the scriptural spine. Try to hold that text in mind as the khutbah continues—everything else the imam says should be illuminating it. If you do not catch the reference, that is one thing to look up afterward.

The practical lesson or call to action

A good khutbah moves from the text to the life. Listen for the bridge: what is the imam asking the congregation to do, stop doing, or reflect on? That single concrete takeaway—even if the rest blurs—is often what Allah intended to reach you that day.

After the prayer

The two rakʿahs of Jumuʿah prayer complete what the khutbah began. Stay for the sunnah prayers if you are able. Spend a few moments in duʿa—the hour of acceptance on Friday is real, and the period after Jumuʿah is among the times scholars mention as hopeful for it.

If something in the khutbah moved you, write it down. One sentence. A verse you want to revisit, a habit you want to build. The khutbah is a weekly recalibration—treat it like one, and Friday will feel like what it was always meant to be: a light at the centre of your week.
