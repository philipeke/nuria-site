How to read Quran with tajweed as a beginner

Nuria · Jul 28, 2026 · 3 min read

There is a moment many of us know well—opening the Quran, wanting to recite beautifully, and feeling uncertain about where to start. Tajweed, the set of rules governing how we pronounce and recite the words of Allah, can seem like a mountain when you are standing at its foot. But here is something worth holding onto: the Prophet ﷺ said, “The one who recites the Quran and is proficient in it will be with the honourable, righteous scribes, and the one who recites it and stumbles over it, finding it difficult, will have a double reward.” You are rewarded for the effort itself.

What is tajweed, really?

Tajweed comes from the Arabic root meaning to improve or make something excellent. In practice, it is a system of pronunciation rules that protects the meaning of the Quran—because in Arabic, a small shift in a vowel or a letter can change a word entirely. The scholars of every madhhab agree that reciting with proper tajweed is an obligation when reciting aloud, and that learning it is a communal duty upon the Muslim community.

As a beginner, your goal is not perfection. Your goal is consistency and sincere effort.

Start with the Arabic letters

Before anything else, you need to be comfortable with the Arabic alphabet and its pronunciation points—what scholars call makhaarij al-huroof, the articulation points of the letters. Several letters in Arabic have no equivalent in other languages, and mispronouncing them changes words.

  • Learn where each letter originates in the mouth and throat.
  • Pay particular attention to letters like ع (ayn), غ (ghayn), ح (ha), خ (kha), and ق (qaf)—these trip up most beginners.
  • Use audio resources and repeat aloud. Reading silently will not build muscle memory.

Find a teacher

This is the single most important step. Tajweed is a transmitted science—it has always been passed from teacher to student, mouth to ear, going back to the Prophet ﷺ himself. A qualified teacher will hear mistakes you cannot hear in yourself.

If you do not have access to a local teacher, online tajweed classes are widely available. Even one session a week with a qualified reciter will advance you faster than months of self-study alone. Ask at your local masjid, or explore trusted online platforms for one-to-one lessons.

Learn the foundational rules gradually

There are four areas beginners usually tackle first:

  • Noon sakinah and tanween rules—covering idghaam, ikhfaa, iqlaab, and izhaar. These govern what happens to the letter ن when it is followed by specific letters.
  • Meem sakinah rules—similar rules applying to a silent م.
  • Madd (elongation)—knowing when and how long to stretch a vowel sound.
  • Qalqalah—the slight echo produced on certain letters when they carry a sukoon.

Do not rush through these. Understand one rule, apply it in your daily recitation, then move to the next.

Read a little every day

Consistency outweighs quantity. Ten minutes of focused recitation daily, applying what you have learned, will build your skills far more reliably than an hour once a week. Start with the shorter surahs you already know. Recite them slowly, paying attention to each rule as it appears.

Listen to recitations

Surround yourself with beautiful, correct recitation. Listening trains your ear to recognise proper tajweed, and your ear must lead your tongue. Scholars such as Sheikh Mahmoud Khalil al-Husary, Sheikh Abdul Basit Abdus Samad, and Sheikh Mishary Rashid Alafasy are widely respected for their clarity and precision—their recordings are a gift to learners.

Be patient with yourself

Tajweed is a lifelong companion, not a box to tick. The greatest reciters in the world still study and refine. Every time you open the Quran, you are in conversation with the words of Allah—and that intention, held sincerely, is where the journey truly begins.
