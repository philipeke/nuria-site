Tahajjud: how to start the night prayer

Nuria · Jul 30, 2026 · 3 min read

What is Tahajjud?

Tahajjud—also known as Qiyam al-Layl—is a voluntary night prayer performed after Isha and before Fajr, ideally after waking from sleep. It is not obligatory, but it holds a place close to the heart of every scholar and practitioner who has tasted it.

Allah addresses the Prophet ﷺ in Surah Al-Isra: "And during part of the night, offer the Tahajjud prayer as an additional prayer for you; perhaps your Lord will raise you to a praised station." (17:79). That maqam mahmud—the praised station—is understood by scholars to be the greatest intercession on the Day of Judgment.

Why it transforms your day

Those who pray Tahajjud regularly speak about it in consistent terms: a clarity that does not come from coffee, a patience that does not come from discipline alone, and a sense of being known by Allah in a way that ordinary daytime rushes rarely allow.

The Prophet ﷺ told us that Allah descends to the lowest heaven in the last third of the night and asks: "Who is calling upon Me, that I may answer them? Who is asking of Me, that I may give to them? Who is seeking My forgiveness, that I may forgive them?" (Bukhari, Muslim). To stand in that window—even for two units—is to place yourself in the most responsive moment of the entire day.

How many rak'ahs?

There is flexibility here, and scholars across all four madhhabs agree on this. The minimum is two rak'ahs. The Prophet ﷺ himself would pray up to thirteen rak'ahs including Witr. Ibn Umar reported that Tahajjud is performed two by two, ending with a single Witr.

  • Beginners: Start with two rak'ahs. Consistency matters far more than quantity.
  • Growing: Move to four, then eight as it becomes part of your night.
  • Witr: End with one, three, five, or seven rak'ahs of Witr—the closing prayer of the night.

A practical way to begin tonight

The biggest barrier is waking up. Here are steps that actually work:

  • Set an alarm for the last third of the night. To find it: take the time between Isha and Fajr, divide by three, and add two-thirds to the Isha time.
  • Sleep with wudu. The Prophet ﷺ recommended this, and many find it makes waking easier and the heart lighter.
  • Keep your prayer mat ready before you sleep. Removing friction matters.
  • Start with Surah Al-Fatiha and a short surah you know well. This is not the night to test your memorization—it is the night to be present.
  • Make dua in sujud. The closest a servant is to Allah is in prostration. Say whatever is in your heart.

What if you miss it?

Do not be harsh with yourself. The Prophet ﷺ said that if a person sleeps through their regular Tahajjud, they should make it up between Fajr and the time the sun rises fully. Missing one night does not break the practice—returning to it does.
