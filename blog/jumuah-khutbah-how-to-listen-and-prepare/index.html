The Jumu'ah khutbah: how to listen and prepare

Nuria · Aug 4, 2026 · 3 min read

Every Friday, across every timezone, Muslims gather for Salat al-Jumu'ah. At the heart of that gathering is the khutbah—two sermons delivered before the prayer. For many of us, the khutbah has become familiar background, something we sit through rather than sit with. But the khutbah was designed by the Sunnah to be the week's most concentrated moment of reminder and renewal.

Why the khutbah matters

Allah says in the Quran: "O you who have believed, when the call to prayer is made on the day of Jumu'ah, then proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade—that is better for you, if you only knew." (Al-Jumu'ah 62:9). The remembrance mentioned here is broader than the prayer alone—it includes the khutbah, the gathering, and the collective orientation of the ummah toward what truly matters.

The khutbah is also a fard (obligation). Across all four madhhabs, it is a condition of the validity of Jumu'ah. This alone tells us something: it is not a preamble. It is part of the act of worship itself.

How to prepare before you arrive

Make ghusl and dress well

The Prophet غ encouraged ghusl on Fridays, applying fragrance, and wearing one's best clothes. This is not vanity—it is a physical expression of honour for the day. When your body is prepared, your mind tends to follow.

Recite Surah al-Kahf

Reading Surah al-Kahf on Friday is a confirmed Sunnah. Many scholars recommend beginning in the morning so that you arrive at the masjid already in a reflective state. The surah's themes—trials of faith, gratitude, the transience of this world—prime the heart for whatever the khatib is about to address.

Send salawat abundantly

Friday is the day of salawat upon the Prophet غ. As you travel to the masjid—whether walking, driving, or commuting—occupy your tongue and heart with this remembrance. It settles the nafs and focuses the intention.

Arrive early

The earlier a person arrives, the greater the reward recorded for each hour of waiting. Early arrival also means you can pray tahiyyat al-masjid, settle into a spot without disturbance, and spend a few quiet minutes in du'a or Quran before the adhan. Arriving as the khutbah begins—or after—means missing both the spiritual preparation and, in the view of most scholars, the full reward.

What to listen for during the khutbah

  • The Quranic ayat and hadith cited. A good khatib anchors the khutbah in textual evidence. Note which verses are quoted—they are worth returning to later in the week.
  • The central reminder. Every khutbah, however long or short, has a core message. Try to name it in one sentence before you leave the masjid. This exercise forces attention and makes the reminder portable.
  • The call to action. Sound khutbahs end with something practical—a habit to build, a wrong to correct, a relationship to mend. Identify it and ask yourself: is this for me this week?

The adab of listening

The scholars are in agreement—across all madhhabs—that speaking during the khutbah, even to tell someone else to be quiet, is disliked and may diminish one's Jumu'ah reward. This is a rare moment that asks for full presence. Put the phone away, not just on silent. Still the body so the heart can move.

If the khutbah is in a language you do not understand, use the time for silent dhikr and du'a rather than letting the mind wander. You are still in the House of Allah, and that presence carries its own weight.

After the prayer

The hour before Maghrib on Friday contains the special time—often identified as the last hour of the afternoon—when du'a is answered. Before you disperse into the rest of your day, pause and ask. The Prophet غ described Friday as a day of 'Eid for the believers. Carry a little of that lightness into the week.
