Sujud al-sahw: correcting mistakes in prayer

Nuria · Jul 27, 2026 · 3 min read

We have all been there: halfway through Asr, you lose count of the rak'at. Or the words of Tashahhud slip from your mind mid-sitting. The heart tightens—did I ruin my prayer?

The answer, most of the time, is no. Allah in His mercy gave us sujud al-sahw—the prostration of forgetfulness—as a way to mend what slipped, without starting over.

What is sujud al-sahw?

Sujud al-sahw consists of two prostrations performed to compensate for an error made during the prayer. The word sahw means heedlessness or forgetfulness—it covers both genuine forgetting and unintentional mistakes in the prayer's structure.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "When one of you is in doubt about his prayer and does not know how many rak'at he has prayed—three or four—he should cast aside doubt and base his prayer on what he is certain of, then perform two prostrations before giving salam." (Muslim)

When does it apply?

The scholars agree that sujud al-sahw addresses three broad categories of error:

  • Adding something unintentionally—such as standing for a fifth rak'ah thinking it is the fourth.
  • Omitting something—such as forgetting the first Tashahhud (the sitting after the second rak'ah).
  • Doubt—being uncertain about the number of rak'at performed.

Importantly, sujud al-sahw does not apply to deliberate errors, nor to forgetting a rukn (pillar) of the prayer entirely—in many cases, if a full pillar is missed, the rak'ah itself must be repeated.

How is it performed?

The basic method is straightforward:

  • After completing the prayer, before or after the final salam, perform two prostrations—each with the normal tasbeeh (Subhana rabbiya al-a'la).
  • Sit up between them as in regular sujud.
  • Then give salam.

Before or after salam? The madhhab differences

This is where the four schools diverge, and it is worth knowing your own madhhab's position while respecting the others:

  • Hanafi: Sujud al-sahw is always performed after salam—the worshipper gives one salam to the right, performs the two prostrations, then completes with another salam.
  • Maliki: It is performed before salam when the error involves an addition, and after salam when it involves an omission.
  • Shafi'i: It is always performed before salam, regardless of the type of error.
  • Hanbali: It is performed before salam in most cases—particularly for omissions—but after salam for certain additions, following specific hadith evidence.

Each position is grounded in authentic narrations from the Prophet ﷺ. The differences reflect genuine scholarly interpretation, not contradiction. Follow your madhhab with confidence.

A word on doubt

If you are genuinely uncertain how many rak'at you have prayed, the majority position (Hanafi, Shafi'i, Hanbali) is to build on the lesser number—the one you are certain of—and then complete the prayer from there, followed by sujud al-sahw. The Maliki school recommends repeating the prayer entirely if doubt frequently occurs.

The spirit behind it

Sujud al-sahw is not a penalty. It is a gift—a reminder that Allah does not demand mechanical perfection from us. The heart that is present, that notices its lapse and seeks to mend it, is exactly the heart the prayer is meant to cultivate.

Prostrate, complete, and carry on. The door of the prayer was never closed to you.
