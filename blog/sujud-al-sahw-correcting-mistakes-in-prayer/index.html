Sujud al-sahw: correcting mistakes in prayer

Nuria · Aug 5, 2026 · 3 min read

What is sujud al-sahw?

Sujud al-sahw—literally, the prostration of forgetfulness—is a remedy built into the prayer itself. When we forget something obligatory, add something extra, or fall into genuine doubt about how many rak’ahs we have prayed, two additional prostrations at the end of the prayer help complete and repair it. It is an act of mercy from Allah; a recognition that our minds wander and our memories slip, and that sincere worship does not have to be abandoned because of an honest mistake.

When does it apply?

Across the four madhhabs there is broad agreement that sujud al-sahw addresses three main situations:

  • Forgetting a wajib (obligatory) element—such as the first tashahhud in prayers with three or four rak’ahs, or sitting between the two prostrations
  • Adding something extra—an unintentional extra rak’ah, ruku’, or sujud
  • Genuine doubt about the number of rak’ahs—not mere whispers (waswas), but real uncertainty about whether you have completed two or three rak’ahs, for example

It is important to note that sujud al-sahw does not compensate for a missed rukn (pillar of prayer), such as missing an entire rak’ah or omitting ruku’ altogether. Those situations require different remedies depending on the madhhab.

How is it performed?

The scholars differ on the exact timing and placement of the prostrations, and these differences are all valid expressions of the Sunnah:

Before the final salam

The Maliki school generally holds that sujud al-sahw for adding extra elements is performed after the salam, while for omissions it is performed before. The Shafi’i school holds that it is always performed before the salam. In both cases, the worshipper makes two prostrations, each with the standard subhanallah, then sits and gives salam.

After the final salam

The Hanafi school holds that sujud al-sahw is performed after the first salam (to the right) but before the second, then closes with tashahhud and salam. The Hanbali position, like the Maliki, depends on whether the error was an addition or an omission—and in cases of addition, it is performed after the salam.

In all four madhhabs, the two prostrations of sujud al-sahw are each accompanied by the same tasbeeh as regular sujud.

Doubt about the number of rak’ahs

If you are genuinely unsure how many rak’ahs you have prayed, the Hanafi position is to repeat the prayer if the doubt is recurrent; otherwise, act on the stronger likelihood. The Maliki, Shafi’i, and Hanbali positions generally advise building on certainty—assuming the lesser number and completing from there—then performing sujud al-sahw before the salam.

A word of reassurance

The Prophet ﷺ himself performed sujud al-sahw and taught his companions to do the same. It is not a sign of carelessness—it is a sign of attentiveness. If you have made a genuine mistake and are unsure which ruling applies to your madhhab, complete your prayer, perform sujud al-sahw as a precaution, and ask a knowledgeable scholar.
