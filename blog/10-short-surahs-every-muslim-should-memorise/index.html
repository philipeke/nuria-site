10 short surahs every Muslim should memorise

Nuria · Aug 1, 2026 · 3 min read

There is something quietly profound about having the words of Allah always with you—not just on a shelf or in an app, but woven into your memory so that they surface in the stillness before Fajr, in a crowded commute, or in a moment of need. The short surahs of Juz Amma are often where that journey begins.

These ten surahs are among the most widely memorised in the Muslim world, and for good reason. They are brief enough to learn within days, yet rich enough to spend a lifetime reflecting on. Every one of them finds its way into salah—and into the heart, if we let it.

The ten surahs

1. Al-Fatiha (The Opening)—7 verses

The foundation of every prayer. Recited in each rakat, Al-Fatiha is sometimes called the mother of the Quran. To memorise it is to carry a conversation with Allah into every moment of salah.

2. Al-Ikhlas (Sincerity)—4 verses

A complete statement of tawhid in four lines. The Prophet ﷺ said it is equal in weight to a third of the Quran. Short to learn; boundless in meaning.

3. Al-Falaq (The Daybreak)—5 verses

A supplication for protection, recited as part of the three protective surahs—known as al-mu'awwidhat—alongside Al-Ikhlas and An-Nas. Many scholars recommend reciting it morning and evening as a daily practice.

4. An-Nas (Mankind)—6 verses

Paired with Al-Falaq, An-Nas seeks refuge in Allah from the subtle whispers that can pull the heart away from what matters. Together these two surahs form a complete shield.

5. Al-Kawthar (Abundance)—3 verses

The shortest surah in the Quran, revealed as a direct comfort to the Prophet ﷺ. Three verses carry gratitude, worship, and certainty in a way that few longer passages match.

6. Al-Asr (Time)—3 verses

Imam al-Shafi'i reportedly said: if people reflected on this surah alone, it would suffice them as a guide. A complete framework for a meaningful life, compressed into three verses.

7. Al-Fil (The Elephant)—5 verses

A reminder of how Allah protected the Kaaba—and by extension, this deen—without the intervention of human power. Reciting it reconnects us to a history of divine protection.

8. Al-Masad (The Palm Fibre)—5 verses

A surah that addresses enmity toward the message of Islam directly and honestly. Its placement in the Quran is a reminder that opposition to truth has always existed—and has never ultimately prevailed.

9. An-Nasr (The Help)—3 verses

Among the last surahs revealed, An-Nasr carries a sense of completion and gratitude. It was understood by the Companions as a signal of the Prophet's ﷺ approaching passing—and taught them to respond to victory not with pride, but with tasbih and istighfar.

10. Al-Maun (Small Kindnesses)—7 verses

A surah that links faith directly to how we treat the vulnerable—the orphan, the one in need. It is a quiet but firm call to make worship real in everyday conduct.

How to begin

The most effective approach is repetition in small doses. Read the transliteration and translation once to understand the meaning, then recite the Arabic aloud—slowly, attentively—several times each day. Many people find it helpful to use one new surah as the qira'a in nafl prayers until it feels settled.

There is no race here. Each surah you carry in your heart is a gift that goes with you everywhere—into your prayers, your supplications, and the quiet moments in between.

May Allah make the Quran easy on our tongues and illuminating in our hearts.
