10 short surahs every Muslim should memorise

Nuria · Aug 6, 2026 · 3 min read

There is something quietly powerful about carrying a surah in your heart. Not just knowing the words on a page, but having them rise naturally in salah, in a moment of worry, or in the still minutes before sleep. Juz’ Amma—the thirtieth and final juz’ of the Qur’an—is where most of us begin, and for good reason. Its surahs are short, rhythmically beautiful, and rich with meaning that repays a lifetime of reflection.

Here are ten surahs worth giving your heart to, whatever your madhab or wherever you are on the path.

The ten surahs

1. Surah Al-Fatiha (The Opening)

Recited in every rak‘ah of every salah, Al-Fatiha is the most repeated passage in the life of a practising Muslim. The scholars of all four madhhabs agree on its centrality. Memorise it with understanding—each line is a conversation with Allah.

2. Surah Al-Ikhlas (Sincerity)

Three short ayat that define the oneness of Allah with unmatched precision. The Prophet ﷺ described it as equivalent in reward to reciting a third of the Qur’an. A surah for dawn, for doubt, and for gratitude alike.

3. Surah Al-Falaq (The Daybreak)

One of the Mu‘awwidhatain—the two surahs of seeking refuge. Recite it morning and evening, after each salah, and before sleep. It is protection made into du‘a.

4. Surah An-Nas (Mankind)

The second of the Mu‘awwidhatain, seeking refuge in Allah as the Lord, King, and God of all people. Together with Al-Falaq, it forms a complete shelter. The Prophet ﷺ would recite both and blow gently into his cupped hands before sleeping.

5. Surah Al-Kawthar (Abundance)

The shortest surah in the Qur’an—three ayat—yet immense in its comfort. Revealed as a direct response to those who sought to wound the Prophet ﷺ, it is a reminder that what Allah gives cannot be taken away.

6. Surah Al-Asr (The Declining Day)

Imam Al-Shafi‘i said: if people pondered only this surah, it would be enough guidance for them. Four short ayat that lay out the entire framework of a life well lived: iman, righteous deeds, and mutual counsel in truth and patience.

7. Surah Al-Fil (The Elephant)

A reminder that entire armies can be undone by the smallest of Allah’s creation. Brief and vivid, it roots the heart in tawakkul—trust that Allah’s protection needs no human explanation.

8. Surah Quraysh

Often read in sequence with Al-Fil, this surah calls Quraysh—and by extension all of us—to gratitude for security and sustenance. It ends with a simple, profound instruction: worship the Lord of this House.

9. Surah Al-Ma’un (Small Kindnesses)

A surah that holds a mirror up to our practice. It asks not only about grand acts of worship but about how we treat the orphan, the poor, and the neighbour. Hard to memorise and then walk past someone in need without it coming to mind.

10. Surah Az-Zalzalah (The Earthquake)

Eight ayat about the Day when the earth gives up its record—and the arresting conclusion that whoever does an atom’s weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom’s weight of evil will see it. A surah for accountability and for hope in equal measure.

A word on the journey

Memorisation is not a race. A single surah held firmly, recited with presence in salah, pondered in quiet moments—that is worth more than a rapid accumulation you cannot feel. Start with what you love. Return to what you find difficult. And know that every moment spent with the words of Allah is itself an act of worship, regardless of how much remains ahead.
