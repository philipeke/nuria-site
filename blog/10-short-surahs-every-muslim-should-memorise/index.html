10 short surahs every Muslim should memorise

Nuria · Jul 28, 2026 · 3 min read

The last section of the Quran—Juz' Amma—is where most of us begin our journey with memorisation. Its surahs are short enough to learn in a sitting, yet so densely meaningful that scholars have spent lifetimes unpacking them. Whether you are reinforcing what you already know or building a stronger foundation, these ten surahs reward every minute you give them.

Why these ten?

They cover the full range of what the Quran asks of us: gratitude, surrender, clarity of belief, protection, and reflection on the world around us. They also appear frequently in salah—knowing them with confidence transforms prayer from recitation into conversation.

The ten surahs

1. Al-Fatiha (The Opening)

Recited in every unit of every prayer, Al-Fatiha is the most repeated passage in the Quran. The Prophet ﷺ called it Umm al-Quran—the mother of the Quran. Its seven verses move from praise, to acknowledgement of divine sovereignty, to a sincere plea for guidance. No surah is more central to the Muslim's daily life.

2. Al-Ikhlas (Sincerity)

Three verses that encapsulate the entirety of tawhid. The Prophet ﷺ said it is equivalent to a third of the Quran in reward. Recite it after fajr and maghrib, and you carry the core of Islamic theology with you through the day.

3. Al-Falaq (The Daybreak)

Together with An-Nas, this surah forms the mu'awwidhatayn—the two surahs of seeking refuge. It is a morning and evening protection, seeking shelter from harm that is hidden and harm that is open. Simple, powerful, prophetically recommended.

4. An-Nas (Mankind)

The final surah of the Quran. It calls on three of Allah's names—Rabb, Malik, Ilah—and seeks protection from the whispering of Shaytan in the chest. The Quran ends, fittingly, with a reminder of where the real struggle lies.

5. Al-Kawthar (Abundance)

The shortest surah in the Quran at three verses, yet among the most consoling. Revealed when the Prophet ﷺ was mocked and grieving, it is a reminder that what Allah gives cannot be taken away. Carry it when your own trials feel relentless.

6. Al-Asr (Time)

Imam Al-Shafi'i said that if people reflected on this surah alone, it would be sufficient guidance. Four verses describing the human condition—and the four qualities that lift a person out of loss: faith, righteous deeds, mutual encouragement toward truth, and mutual encouragement toward patience.

7. Al-Fil (The Elephant)

A reminder that Allah's protection of His sacred house came not through armies but through small birds carrying stones of baked clay. History is full of powerful forces that crumbled, and this surah keeps that perspective alive.

8. Al-Quraysh (Quraysh)

Often paired with Al-Fil, it calls the Quraysh to gratitude—for the security and provision they enjoyed, and for the House that granted them their status. It is a surah about recognising blessings before they disappear.

9. Al-Ma'un (Small Kindnesses)

One of the most searching surahs in the Quran. It defines the one who denies the faith not by theological error, but by neglecting the orphan, ignoring the poor, and performing prayer without presence or purpose. A weekly examination of the heart.

10. Al-Nasr (Divine Help)

The last surah revealed in full. It speaks of victory and of what comes after—not celebration, but increased glorification and seeking of forgiveness. A profound lesson that success in this life calls for greater humility, not less.

A practical note on memorisation

Learn one surah at a time. Read its meaning, listen to a recitation you find moving, then repeat each verse aloud until it settles. Use it in your voluntary prayers before moving on. The goal is not speed—it is familiarity deep enough that the words feel like your own.

These ten surahs are not a ceiling. They are a door.
